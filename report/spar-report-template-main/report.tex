\section{Introduction}

Understanding how large language models lie is becoming more important as their capabilities increase.
One way of evaluating large langauge models has been to use what is called a black-box evaluator, a different large-langauge model that checks the output for falsehoods. 
However, such models have limitations and can be computationally expensive.
White-box detectors complement this approach by applying simpler statistical models to the internal datastreams of LLMs.
As LLMs are increasingly used as agents in helping with research, booking-systems and coding, there is a growing need for evaluating these methods in settings where they are most likely to be used.
Previous research has found th lie-detection methods often fail to generalize to new datasets or model families.
Defining what a lie is is also methodologically fraught with risk. 
As such, creating correct labels for datasets can often result in noisy evaluations.
To address this problem in evaluating lie detection, we have tested two previously untested environments for evaluating lie detection methods in large language models over X many different model families, sizes and probe architectures.
